\section{Conclusion}

\sys frames retrieval infrastructure for agentic AI as an auditable deployment decision rather than a leaderboard. The key idea is to report a configuration only after it passes a fixed adapter contract, then expose workload quality, post-insert top-10 retrievability, \maxrowp latency, normalized context-cost proxy, budget stability, objective sensitivity, and paired margins in one decision card. The archived local evidence shows why that audit matters: the strict 200 ms policy selects FAISS CPU/bge-small for S1--S3, but alternative objectives change decisions, S1/S2 are tail-latency tie-breaks, the S3 pgvector aggregate edge is indistinguishable under matched queries, and short B0 runs do not preserve all B2 decisions. The supported conclusion is bounded but actionable: local retrieval-memory deployment choices are more credible when their conformance, objective, budget, and decision margins are explicit.

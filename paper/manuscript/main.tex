\begin{abstract}
Modern agentic AI systems use retrieval infrastructure as operational memory: they repeatedly read, write, and pack retrieved evidence into model contexts under latency and cost constraints. Deployment choices, however, are often made from quality-only leaderboards or from system benchmarks that omit agent-facing freshness and context-cost effects. We present \sys, a conformance-gated, single-node benchmark for comparing retrieval engines under three agentic workloads: single-hop corpus retrieval, streaming memory with post-insert freshness probes, and multi-hop evidence retrieval. The benchmark evaluates four reportable engines, two local embedding models, and three budget levels, then separates strict-latency deployment decisions from unconstrained-cost and quality-first choices. On the archived portable run, a 200 ms worst-case p99 constraint selects FAISS CPU with bge-small for all three workloads, but the decision margins differ: S1 and S2 are cost/quality ties resolved by latency, while S3 has lower cost and higher evidence coverage than the next strict-cost alternative. Paired audits rule out two tempting overclaims: Qdrant does not hide an S2 quality/freshness win in the larger same-machine check, and the S3 pgvector quality-first margin is not substantive under matched queries. The results also show why benchmark budgets should be reported explicitly: B0-to-B2 top-1 decisions change for single-hop and streaming workloads, whereas the multi-hop workload keeps the same top-1 recommendation despite low full-rank correlation. \sys therefore does not claim universal stability; it provides a reproducible protocol for deciding when local benchmark evidence is stable enough for deployment.
\end{abstract}
